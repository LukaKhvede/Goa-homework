Formula 1 - History

What is Formula 1?

Formula 1 is the highest class of international single-seater auto racing sanctioned by the FIA. The first World Championship season was held in 1950. Since then it has grown into one of the most watched sports in the world.

The sport features the fastest road-course racing cars in the world. Teams spend hundreds of millions of dollars each year developing their cars. The races take place on purpose-built circuits and on closed public roads around the world.

Each season consists of a series of races known as Grands Prix. Points are awarded to the top finishers in each race. The driver with the most points at the end of the season wins the World Drivers Championship. Teams also compete for the Constructors Championship.

Formula 1 has seen many legendary drivers, iconic teams, and unforgettable moments over its 70+ year history. From the early days of front-engined cars to today's hybrid power units, the sport has constantly evolved.

F1 Legends

Michael Schumacher

Michael Schumacher is the most successful Formula 1 driver of his era. The German driver won 7 World Championship titles, which was a record for many years. He drove for teams including Benetton, Ferrari, and Mercedes. His dominance with Ferrari from 2000 to 2004 is considered one of the greatest runs in F1 history. He won 91 races in his career.

Ayrton Senna

Ayrton Senna was a Brazilian racing driver who is widely regarded as one of the greatest Formula 1 drivers of all time. He won three World Championship titles in 1988, 1990, and 1991, all with McLaren. Senna was known for his exceptional skill in wet weather conditions and his intense rivalry with Alain Prost. His tragic death at the 1994 San Marino Grand Prix shocked the world.

Niki Lauda

Niki Lauda was an Austrian Formula 1 driver and aviation entrepreneur. He won the World Championship three times, in 1975, 1977, and 1984. His most remarkable story is his survival after a horrific crash at the 1976 German Grand Prix at the Nurburgring. Despite suffering severe burns, he returned to racing just six weeks later. His rivalry with James Hunt in 1976 is the stuff of legend.

Juan Manuel Fangio

Juan Manuel Fangio was an Argentine racing driver who dominated the first decade of Formula 1. He won five World Championship titles between 1951 and 1957, driving for four different teams. His record of five championships stood for 45 years until Schumacher broke it. Many consider him the greatest F1 driver of all time.
